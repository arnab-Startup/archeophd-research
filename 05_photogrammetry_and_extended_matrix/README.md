## 1. The Geometric Reality of Photogrammetric Sensing

### 1.1 Mathematical Physics of Structure-from-Motion (SfM)
Structure-from-Motion photogrammetry and terrestrial LiDAR compute coordinates of reflective surfaces by solving bundle adjustment equations over optical rays:
* SfM produces a 2D triangular manifold (surface boundary) embedded in 3D Euclidean space.
* **CRMarchaeo Ontological Mapping:**
  - A photogrammetric mesh represents an **`A3 Stratigraphic Interface`** (depositional surface or destruction cut) or an **`A10 Excavation Interface`** (the temporary physical surface exposed by the excavator on a given day).
* **The Volumetric Fallacy:** Optical photogrammetry cannot penetrate opaque soil. It cannot sense the internal density, composition, or subterranean volume of an archaeological layer. Therefore, photogrammetry **never directly records an `A2 Stratigraphic Volume Unit`**.

### 1.2 Deriving Volumes via Differential DEMs
An `A2 Stratigraphic Volume Unit` can only be computed mathematically as the difference between two successive interface scans:
$$V(\text{A2}) = \text{Volume Between}(\text{Interface}_{t_1}, \text{Interface}_{t_2})$$

## 2. The Photogrammetric Mesh Intersection Fallacy

### 2.1 The Myth in v1.0 Documentation
ArchaeoPhD AI v1.0 claimed that "geometric mesh intersections between photogrammetric scans directly prove impossible stratigraphy or author error."

### 2.2 Geomatics Reality in Trench Practice
In real excavation geomatics, daily photogrammetric models are registered into site coordinates using ground control points (GCPs) measured with total stations or RTK GNSS:
* **Registration Residuals:** Even under ideal field conditions, registration residuals range between **5 mm and 20 mm**.
* **Surface Roughness & Decimation:** Soil crevices, shadows, and point-cloud decimation noise regularly cause mesh triangles from Day 2 to penetrate mesh triangles from Day 1.
* **Engine Rule:** Mesh collisions must be logged as **Photogrammetric Registration Residuals / Noise**, not as impossible stratigraphy.

## 3. Extended Matrix (EM) & s3dgraphy Integration

### 3.1 What is Extended Matrix?
Developed by **Emanuel Demetrescu** (CNR-ISPC, Italy), the Extended Matrix (EM) is the international archaeological standard that connects stratigraphic sequences (Harris Matrices) with 3D virtual reconstructions.

### 3.2 Key Concept: Virtual Stratigraphic Units (USV)
While physical deposits are recorded as Stratigraphic Units (SU), hypothetical elements that have eroded away (e.g., a missing roof, collapsed wall, or timber floor) are modeled as **Virtual Stratigraphic Units (USV)**:
* Each USV is linked to formal paradata: the chain of historical and comparative evidence that justifies its 3D geometry.

### 3.3 The `s3dgraphy` JSON Schema
ArchaeoPhD AI v2.0 exports extracted stratigraphic relationships directly into the **`s3dgraphy`** JSON schema:
* Allows 3D digital modelers in Blender, ATON, and EMStudio to import the complete stratigraphic DAG generated by ArchaeoPhD AI without manual data re-entry.
