## 2. Sector 1: US Cultural Resource Management (CRM) & Section 106

### 2.1 Market Drivers & Size
* **Market Volume:** **\$1.46B to \$1.85B annual revenue** across 1,300+ commercial environmental and cultural resource firms in the United States.
* **Statutory Mandate:** Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act (NHPA; 54 U.S.C. 306108) and the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA). Any infrastructure undertaking receiving federal funding or permits (interstate highways, high-speed rail, pipelines, solar/wind farms, 5G towers) must complete a cultural resource review.
* **Federal Catalyst:** The Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA) injected \$1.2T into federal infrastructure, triggering unprecedented demand for compliance archaeology.

### 2.2 The Structural PhD Labor Shortage (Altschul et al.)
In their landmark study (*"Forecast for the US CRM Industry and Job Market, 2022–2031"*, published in *Advances in Archaeological Practice*), Jeffrey Altschul and colleagues revealed a critical labor bottleneck:
* The US CRM industry must hire **11,000 new professionals** between 2022 and 2031 (including over **8,000 archaeologists**).
* **Advanced Degree Mandate:** Under the Secretary of the Interior's Professional Qualification Standards, roughly **70% of professional CRM roles legally require an advanced graduate degree (Master's or PhD)**.
* **The Deficit:** US anthropology and archaeology graduate programs produce only $\sim 325$ MA/PhD graduates per year ($\sim 3,250$ over the decade).
* **The Deficit Gap:** The industry faces an unbridgeable shortage of over **4,500 advanced-degree professionals**.

### 2.3 Value Proposition & Pricing
* CRM firms (AECOM, WSP, SWCA, PaleoWest, ERM) cannot hire their way out of this shortage.
* ArchaeoPhD AI automates preliminary desk-based literature reviews, synthesizing thousands of pages of historical excavation reports and archaeological site surveys into compliance drafts in hours instead of weeks.
* **Pricing Model:** \$50,000 to \$250,000 annual enterprise SaaS seat licenses per firm.

## 3. Sector 2: European Preventive Archaeology (The Valletta Convention)

### 3.1 Regulatory Framework
* Signed in 1992 under the Council of Europe, the **Valletta Convention** legally established the **"polluter-pays" principle** across Europe. Private property developers must finance archaeological evaluation and rescue excavations prior to ground disturbance.

### 3.2 Public Budgets (INRAP Case Study)
* France's National Institute for Preventive Archaeological Research (*Institut national de recherches archéologiques préventives*, INRAP) operates on an annual budget of $\sim €190\text{M}$ funded by the *Taxe d'archéologie préventive* (TAP) and *Redevance d'archéologie préventive* (RAP).
* Over 10-year windows, French preventive archaeology represents **$>€1.5\text{B}$** in direct expenditures.
* Similar systems operate across the UK (MOLA, Oxford Archaeology), Germany (regional *Landesämter*), and Italy (*Soprintendenze*).

### 3.3 Commercial Strategy
* Multi-year B2G enterprise contracts (€100K–€500K+) to digitize regional excavation archives and eliminate the backlog of unindexed "grey literature" delaying commercial urban developments.

## 4. Sector 3: Space Archaeology & Satellite Remote Sensing

* **Market Growth:** Earth observation and geospatial analytics are expanding from **\$1.8B (2025) to \$4.2B by 2034 at a 9.8% CAGR**.
* **The Commercial Bottleneck:** Satellite operators (Planet Labs, Maxar, Airbus Intelligence, Orbital Insight) deploy automated computer vision on multispectral and SAR satellite imagery to identify buried archaeological structures and subsurface soil marks.
* However, an analyst must manually sift through decades of historical literature to determine whether the detected anomaly is a known archaeological site or a modern feature.
* **Commercial Strategy:** OEM API licensing (\$100K–\$400K/year) connecting satellite computer vision detections to automated literature entity resolution.
